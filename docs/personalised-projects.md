# OddHobb Personalised Projects

I think this is potentially the strongest category we've uncovered for
OddHobb: not just gifts made for someone, but projects made specifically
for someone to enjoy making.

The distinction is subtle but commercially important.

A normal personalised gift says, "I had this made for you."

A personalised project says, "I found something you'd love making, and I
had the entire experience designed around you."

Imagine telling Oddy:

> "My girlfriend loves frogs, miniature things, and our cat Pickles. She's
> never knitted before, but she loves weird little crafts."

OddHobb could propose a beginner-friendly frog-and-Pickles crochet
project, a miniature bedroom for Pickles, or a custom frog-themed charm kit.

Each is a real build with its own design, exact materials, printed booklet
and video guide.

The customer doesn't have to choose the craft first. OddHobb recommends it.

## 1. The categories I'd explore

1. **Charm Lab — Make your own accessories.** Target: £15–25. Low
   production difficulty. Personalise the beads, letters, colours, and one
   signature 3D-printed charm based on someone's pet, hobby or inside
   joke. The kit builds two or three charms for bags, keys or phones.
2. **Tiny Worlds — Build your own miniature room.** Target: £35–65.
   Medium difficulty. Upload room photographs, pets and favourite objects.
   OddHobb creates a matching miniature world with pre-cut walls,
   furniture, wallpaper and a bespoke illustrated assembly guide.
   Eventually every kit has a digital twin and optional AI inhabitant.
3. **Stitch Your Person / Pet.** Target: £20–35. Low–medium difficulty.
   Photograph → simplified stitchable artwork → colour-matched thread →
   printed pattern → complete kit. Established market (custom kits ~£23).
4. **Crochet Your Character.** Target: £22–38. Medium difficulty. Your pet,
   favourite animal or creature becomes a custom crochet pattern. Box
   contains measured yarn, tools and a step-by-step guide. Harder to
   automate because patterns must actually work.
5. **Tiny Book Studio.** Target: £18–30. Low difficulty. Someone's
   photographs, sayings, stories or memories become a tiny book they
   assemble and bind themselves. Excellent for couples, friendship gifts
   and family Christmas presents.
6. **Miniature Clay Studio.** Target: £18–32. Low–medium difficulty.
   Recreate someone's favourite food, pet or object in miniature.
   Personalised templates, portioned clay, sculpting tools, colour recipes
   and detailed instructions.

## 2. The opportunity isn't just personalising the final object

Four layers of personalisation:

| Layer          | Example                                                    | Difficulty |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Appearance     | Favourite colours, name, photographs                       | Easy       |
| Subject        | Build your actual pet, room, car or favourite object       | Medium     |
| Experience     | Beginner-friendly, short sessions, preferred instructions  | Medium     |
| Entire project | Invent a unique build based on someone's interests         | Hard       |

Most personalised craft businesses stop at the first two. OddHobb could
eventually do all four. The especially interesting layer is personalising
the experience: the same miniature room as a 45-minute simple version or
a detailed six-hour version. Pre-sorted components and short resumable
steps for strugglers; complexity for enthusiasts.

## 3. How a personalised project gets compiled

The trick is to make the project template reusable while its output is
unique. Canonical Tiny Room Template: fixed dimensions, wall connectors,
floor, mounting points, furniture interfaces, validated assembly
procedure. Personalisation inputs: photos of bedroom + pet + favourite
objects + desired style. Project Compiler: select compatible assets →
generate textures → adapt CAD → freeze exact parts → render instructions.
Output: exact parts, bags, materials, printed booklet (physical kit) +
editable scene, source files, digital twin (digital project) + step
guides (experience) + compatible future upgrades.

The key engineering rule: physical parts, assembly diagrams, packing list
and digital model must derive from the same frozen project revision.
Otherwise personalisation breaks things.

## 4. Make gifting the primary user experience

Don't make people configure materials, select processes or write prompts.
The interface should feel like Oddy is doing the shopping: who is it for,
what's unusual about them, budget, craft experience → find their project.
The real implementation also accepts photographs, produces actual renders
of compatible kits, and lets the customer buy through Shopify.

## 5. The business model gets better with every reusable template

Project family → verified base kit → interchangeable components →
personalised output. Most of a charm kit is standard inventory (chains,
connectors, cord, beads, clasp); the order needs only one bespoke
3D-printed piece. Expanding into keychains, phone charms, shoelace charms
and ornaments reuses materials and design assets. Tiny Worlds equivalent:
universal room shell, furniture library, custom printable surfaces,
optional bespoke objects.

Priority: bag/phone charms (1), mini books (2), stitch-from-photo (3),
tiny rooms (4), clay (5), crochet figures (6). Develop Tiny Worlds
alongside the cheaper kits, but don't make it carry early cash flow until
manufacturing is validated.

## 6. Gifts that become traditions

Retain purchased project specs (with consent) so future gifts stay
compatible: bedroom now, library extension next Christmas. No
subscriptions needed initially — just compatibility. Each completed
project becomes another object the agent can recognise digitally.

## Strategy: three propositions

OddHobb Gifts — Make it for them (current cards, figures, ornaments).
OddHobb Projects — Make it with them (creative kits, everything included).
OddHobb Worlds — Make it their own (modular environments, digital twins).

Next practical experiment: a personalised charm-making kit + a
personalised miniature room. The charm kit tests the bespoke-making
premium; the room tests the bigger worlds proposition.
